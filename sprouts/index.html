The app

One app for the whole team

Every sprout is one ongoing conversation. Talk to any of them, open its computer to watch it work, and take over when you need to.

What a sprout has

Four things make a sprout

Sprouts are not chat tabs. Each one keeps who it is, what it knows and what it does between conversations.

SOUL.md

A soul

A short file sets its personality and how it works. Every edit is versioned, so you can roll any change back.

MEMORY.md

A memory

It remembers what matters and tidies its notes overnight. Facts it picks up from the web, email or other bots are kept apart in quarantine.

Rhythms

Rhythms

Routines that run on their own schedule, like a morning digest, a weekly check or a reminder you would otherwise forget.

Its own computer

A computer

A private Linux computer with a desktop and a browser. Watch its screen live, and take over when you want to.

Working together

Sprouts help each other

  • They talk. A sprout can message another or ask it for help. The exchange appears in both chats, and you can join in.
  • They share a shed. One shared folder holds the files the whole team works on.
  • They know when to stop. Loop guards pause two sprouts that keep messaging each other and ask you what to do.

Could you sort this week’s receipts?

Done. The summary is in the shed.

The cast

Fifteen vegetables, six colours each

Every sprout gets a vegetable of its own. Each one comes in six colours, so a team can hold three carrots and still tell them apart.

Tap a sprout to try its colours.

You stay in charge

Helpful, and fenced in

The Sprouts app on your Mac is the boundary. It holds the keys, asks before connector actions and decides what each computer can reach.

Secrets stay secret

When a sprout needs a password or key, it asks in a private card. Your answer goes straight to the vault on your Mac. The value never appears in the chat or the model’s context.

You approve connector actions

Connector actions that send, post or change something wait for your approval. The check runs outside the bot, so no bot can approve for you.

A fence around each computer

Each sprout’s computer reaches the internet only through Sprouts. Local-network access is set to Ask by default, so a sprout asks before reaching a device on your network. You can turn it Off or On.

A reviewed catalog

Connectors and skills come from a signed, reviewed catalog. Anything you add yourself is marked unreviewed, and updates show what changed before they apply.

Models and status

Bring the models you already use

Sprouts works with API keys, local models through Ollama or LM Studio, and subscription sign-ins whose terms allow it. Each sprout has its own model and a fallback.

The Mac app is in beta for Apple silicon. It is signed with a Developer ID, notarized by Apple, and updates itself in the background, asking before it restarts. Phone apps and Linux support are in development.

Sprouts is separate from the hosted Pea API. The Pea console linked at the top of this page is for the Pea API, not for Sprouts.

Plant your first sprout

  1. Download the beta and open the zip.
  2. Move Sprouts to your Applications folder and open it.
  3. Connect a model and plant your first sprout. Sprouts sets up its own computers; there is no Docker to install.
