Keys and privacy

Models and apps

A persona you sign off on

Each sprout’s identity and voice live in its persona. When a sprout wants to change it, you see the proposal and allow or decline it. Earlier versions are kept, so you can restore one.

Memory that checks with you

Sprouts remember what matters. Anything learned from the web or from another sprout waits under Needs your review until you confirm it or tell the sprout to forget it.

Rhythms for recurring work

Schedule work in plain words: a morning brief on weekdays at 07:00, a weekly check, a reminder. Edit, pause or remove a rhythm whenever you like. After downtime, it catches up once.

A journal you can search

Find a moment again. The journal searches everything a sprout has said, remembered and done.

Settings for each sprout

Give every sprout its own name, model, access and secrets. Pause a sprout to stop its current reply and hold new work until you resume.

Plant a sprout in seconds

Pick a vegetable, give it a name and choose a model. You can rename it later.

Secrets in a private card

When a sprout needs a token, it asks in a card like this. The value goes to the vault on your Mac: the sprout can use it but never sees it. Keep it for that sprout, and revoke it later.

Sign in without sharing passwords

Connect 1Password, Bitwarden or Proton Pass. Sprouts sign in to websites without ever seeing your passwords, and only after you pick a login in a sign-in card.

You choose what to share

Crash reports are on unless you turn them off, and messages, files and known secrets are removed first. Sharing agent activity is off unless you turn it on.

Bring your subscriptions

Connect Codex with your ChatGPT account, Claude, or Antigravity with a Google account, then pick one for each new sprout. Cursor, Grok, Muse and Pi are coming.

API keys, local models or your own server

Use a key from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, xAI, Moonshot, Meta and others, a local model, or a server you run. Keys stay in the vault on your Mac.

Know what is left

See how much of each subscription is left and when it resets. When a limit is reached, the sprout tells you when it will be back.

Skills and connections

Add skills and connect apps such as GitHub, Atlassian, PostHog and Hugging Face, for all your sprouts or just one. Entries from the signed catalog are reviewed; anything you add yourself is marked Unreviewed.

And also

Small things that add up

  • Live status everywhere

    Thinking, Working, Needs your approval: the sidebar, the garden and your iPhone show what each sprout is doing.

  • Stop any reply

    A Stop button sits in the composer while a sprout works.

  • Messages wait for you

    If the connection drops, a message waits in the outbox and sends itself when your computer is back, for up to an hour.

  • Photos and files

    Drop files into a chat on your Mac. On iPhone, add photos, the camera or files from one +.

  • Files in replies

    Open the files a sprout names straight from the chat, with previews for PDF, Word, Excel and photos.

  • Your order, everywhere

    Drag sprouts into the order you like. It follows you to your iPhone.

  • Keyboard shortcuts

    ⌘K jumps to any sprout, ⌘1–9 opens one, ⌘N plants a new one and ⌃⌘S toggles the sidebar.

  • Light and dark

    Sprouts follows your system appearance on Mac and iPhone.

  • Always on

    Sprouts keeps your Mac awake while it runs. Pause all from the menu bar whenever you need a break.

Working together

Sprouts help each other

  • They talk. A sprout can message another or ask it for help. The exchange appears in both chats, and you can join in.
  • You can see who talks. Each sprout’s settings show which other sprouts it may message.
  • They know when to stop. Loop guards pause two sprouts that keep messaging each other and ask you what to do.

You stay in charge

Helpful, and fenced in

The Sprouts app on your Mac is the boundary. It holds the keys, asks before actions in your connected apps by default and decides what each computer can reach.

Secrets stay secret

When a sprout needs a password or key, it asks in a private card. Your answer goes straight to the vault on your Mac. The value never appears in the chat or the model’s context.

You approve app actions

Actions in a connected app ask first by default. Set the ones you trust, like reading pull requests, to Always allow. The check runs outside the bot, so no bot can approve for you.

A fence around each computer

Each sprout’s computer reaches the internet only through Sprouts. Local-network access is set to Ask by default, so a sprout asks before reaching a device on your network. You can turn it Off or On.

A reviewed catalog

Connectors and skills come from a signed, reviewed catalog. Anything you add yourself is marked unreviewed, and skill updates show what changed before they apply.

Models and requirements

Bring the models you already use

Each sprout gets its own model and up to four fallbacks, so work carries on when one runs out.

Requirements

  • A Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 13 or newer
  • A 304 MB download. Sprouts sets up its own computers; there is no Docker to install
  • Signed with a Developer ID, notarized by Apple, and updates itself
  • The iPhone app signs in with the same Vej account
  • Android and Linux are in development

Sprouts is separate from the hosted Pea API. The Pea console in the menu is for Pea.
