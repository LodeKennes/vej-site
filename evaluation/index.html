Calibration and confidence distribution

Overall negative log-likelihood is 0.589448. The summed Brier score is 0.313870. Descriptive ten-bin expected calibration error (ECE) is 0.053473. These figures use temperature 1, with no post-hoc calibration. Decisions with maximum probability ≥ 0.9 cover 58.48% of the test set with an error rate of 5.53%. The 0.8 to 0.9 confidence bin shows 59.22% accuracy at 85.16% mean confidence. The hosted runtime divides logits by 1.334 before the softmax. That temperature was fitted on 2,026 held-out calibration decisions and checked on 1,016 further decisions. It does not change the published exact-match accuracy, and it was not fit on this test.

Pea and Jev on the same rows.

Jev is TypeSafe's hosted decision model and API. To evaluate how Pea compares with Jev's decision contract, hosted Jev 1.13 was tested against the exact same 14,286 held-out decisions. Pea achieved 76.60% exact accuracy (10,943 correct). Hosted Jev 1.13 achieved 71.76% (10,251 correct) with its documented rounding adjustment. Pea leads overall and on Choice and Score. Jev leads on Noul. The difference is 4.84 percentage points on this test. It does not establish general superiority, broader transfer or statistical significance.

How it was compared

The comparison matched dataset hash, row order, question identifiers, and candidate ordering. Hosted Jev returned 14,188 strictly valid responses. On these rows, Jev achieved 71.95% accuracy and Pea achieved 76.80%. The other 98 probability maps summed to 0.99 because of two-decimal output precision. This is one fixed test, not Jev's own benchmark. Speed and cost were not compared.

Pea against prompted open models.

Our paper compares Pea with open 2B, 4B and 9B instruct models from the same family as Pea's backbone, asked for probabilities in the two usual ways: reading the probabilities of answer letters, or asking for a JSON object of probabilities. Every system answered the same 14,286 test decisions. The comparison was pre-registered before any of these models ran; Pea's own results were already known. Pea is ahead of every prompted model, including a 9B model.

Exact accuracy and log loss (NLL, lower is better). Invalid answers count as wrong. The 9B model ran with FP8 weights and is reported outside the pre-registered tests.

What the paper found

Prompted models fail in recognisable ways. Three of the four 2B and 4B setups give the same answer to every, or almost every, question about whether an action's required inputs are known. Letter answers follow the order of the options: reversing the options changed the 2B model's answer on 36% of Choice decisions, and Pea's on 0.2%. Written probabilities are often invalid or put all probability on one answer.

Each outcome is scored independently, with the conversation read once per outcome. With prefix caching, Pea served 9.38 decisions per second at a median of 102 ms. The prompted open 2B model in letter mode served 20.88 per second at 57 ms. These measurements show the accuracy and throughput tradeoff on this test set.

Comparison scope: Pea was trained for these decisions; the generative models were prompted without fine-tuning on the same data. Results cover one English test set, with accuracy reported separately for each question type.

Training

Vej trains rank-16 LoRA adapters and a native FP32 scalar decision head on a frozen pretrained text backbone (revision 15852e8). The original generative pretraining objective does not determine Vej's decision-oriented output interface.

  1. Public pilot. 4,096 training and 256 development decisions. Two epochs, 512 updates, selected at step 256.
  2. Expanded training. 32,768 training and 2,048 development decisions. Two epochs, selected at step 1,280 with validation NLL 0.636590.
  3. First 100k stage. 100,002 training and 28,572 validation decisions. The stage ended early; the selected saved checkpoint was step 1,024 with validation NLL 0.492022.
  4. Recovery stage. Initialized with a fresh optimizer state, seed 43, across one shuffled epoch of the same corpus (6,251 updates). Step 6,144 was selected by lowest pooled validation NLL of 0.458497.
  5. Final evaluation. The selected checkpoint was tested against all 14,286 frozen test decisions on 26 September 2026.

Recovery repeated example exposure rather than performing an exact optimizer resume. Training stages overlap, so their counts cannot be summed to claim unique volume.

Hosted runtime

The hosted API is live in limited early access, by request. It serves Pea’s native reference runtime for text only; image input is refused. Hosted Choice, Noul and Score probabilities use temperature 1.334, fitted on 2,026 held-out calibration decisions and checked on 1,016 further decisions. That scale does not make a stated probability a guaranteed frequency on a new workflow. Capacity is limited, with no uptime guarantee yet. Published evaluations describe the native checkpoint at temperature 1, not a separate hosted evaluation.

If hosted Pea moves to vLLM, probabilities may differ from the native reference by up to about 0.05 (five percentage points) in development checks. Served-runtime temperature fitting and separate held-out verification would be needed. This development tolerance is not a claim of native equivalence or a universal error bound.
