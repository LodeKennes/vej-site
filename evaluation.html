Checkpoint selection

How the checkpoint got here.

Vej trains a native scalar decision head and LoRA adapters on the frozen Qwen3.5-2B text backbone. Its original generative pretraining does not make Vej’s output generative.

  1. Public pilot. 4,096 training and 256 development decisions; two epochs, 512 updates, selected step 256.
  2. Expanded training. 32,768 training and 2,048 development decisions; two epochs, selected step 1,280 with validation NLL 0.636590.
  3. First 100k stage. 100,002 training and 28,572 validation decisions. A reboot interrupted training. The saved selected checkpoint was step 1,024, NLL 0.492022.
  4. Recovery. Fresh optimizer, seed 43, one shuffled epoch of the same corpus: 6,251 updates. Step 6,144 was selected by lowest pooled validation NLL, 0.458497.
  5. Final evaluation. The selected checkpoint was tested on all 14,286 frozen decisions on 26 September 2026.

Recovery repeats example exposure and is not an exact optimizer resume. These stages overlap; their counts cannot be added to claim unique training data. A larger 530,762-row preparation pool was screened, not all trained on.

Vej workflows are authored security, agent-trace, invoice and customer-service policy worlds. Public sources supply NLI relations, intent labels, comparative preferences, human ratings and supplied-service dialogue state. Original labels were not universally re-adjudicated.

Roles & exposure

A split is a protocol.

Training updates parameters. Validation repeatedly selects checkpoints by pooled NLL. The final test was evaluated after checkpoint selection, with no temperature fitting or checkpoint selection on those results. No fitted calibration artifact is applied.

These are Vej’s roles derived from acquired publisher training payloads, not the publishers’ official benchmark splits. Test comprises 5,000 HelpSteer1 ratings and 9,286 SGD decisions. SGD Flights/Hotels families supply test; Events/Services/Homes supply validation. Both source datasets also supply training decisions.

Dialogue and prompt/response grouping, deterministic selection, length screens and exact overlap checks preserve specific boundaries. They do not prove conceptual independence. HelpSteer and HH prompt/response components are joined; new conflicting groups are quarantined.

Known exposure: twelve inherited WANLI validation decisions have earlier training-lineage exposure. Another historical overlap component retains 19 training decisions, without new held-out selections. Backbone pretraining overlap is unknown. One test example was viewed during comparator setup. The complete test is now exposed for future development.

The training primitives are 68,719 Choice / 13,771 Noul / 17,512 Score. Validation contains 19,526 / 4,182 / 4,864. These differ from the final mixture. Validation accuracy of 81.70% and final accuracy of 76.60% are not matched-distribution measurements.

A fresh clone cannot yet reconstruct the entire r5 corpus: parts of preparation bind previously acquired local data and role locks. Original source links and frozen hashes establish provenance, not a fully portable reproduction recipe.

Hosted comparison / same r5 test

Vej and Jev, side by side.

The hosted Jev 1.13 run used the same 14,286 decisions. Response-format validity and prediction accuracy are reported separately.

Vej leads overall; Jev leads on Noul.

Vej: 10,943 correct. Adjusted Jev: 10,251 correct. The overall difference is 4.84 percentage points on this fixed sample, not a general superiority or statistical significance claim.

Rounding adjustment is supplemental.

All 98 flagged responses had complete maps totaling 0.99. Dividing eligible maps by their total restores normalization without changing argmax. No responses were retried or raw records overwritten.

The original strict result stays visible.

99.31% is response-format validity, not accuracy. Jev returned 14,188 strictly valid responses out of 14,286. Its strict-valid accuracy was 71.95%; Vej scored 76.80% on those exact same 14,188 rows. Counting all 98 strict failures wrong gives Jev 71.45% on all 14,286 rows.

On the strict-valid subsets, Jev scored 83.09% Choice (6,174 rows), 93.65% Noul (3,089) and 44.37% Score (4,925). The table above restores all 98 eligible rows: 23 Choice and 75 Score, with no unrecoverable responses.

What makes this comparable—and what limits it.

The dataset hash, row order, IDs, targets and ordered candidates match. One original question was sent per hosted request, with full state and no local truncation. Noul p becomes [p, 1−p]; both models use first-candidate argmax ties and externally computed zero-based Score expectations.

The arithmetic rule was fixed after collection launched: retain all other contract checks, require a complete finite map in [0,1], positive total, values within 1e−12 of two decimals and |total−1| ≤ K×0.005 + 1e−12. Only eligible strict failures are rescaled. This is not the predeclared primary result.

Jev’s two-decimal probabilities can create ties and zeros. Renormalizing does not restore lost precision, so probability metrics need care. Hosted timings include networking, rate limits and retries; they do not establish a speed or cost comparison. The shared-source and exposure limitations above still apply.

Development diagnostics

The tool gap remains.

Separate reused suites measured 60/64 complete workflow actions, including two critical wrong actions; 30/48 harder tool cases, with nine incorrect calls among 31 ready predictions; and 4/12 original tool cases, with three incorrect calls among seven ready predictions.

These diagnostics are not fresh final tests. Supplied-service intent and input-completeness questions do not measure global tool routing, execution permission or a successful real-world call.
