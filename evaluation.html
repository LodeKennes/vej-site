Checkpoint selection

How the checkpoint was trained.

Vej trains rank-16, alpha-32 LoRA adapters and a native FP32 scalar decision head on a frozen Qwen3.5-2B text backbone (revision 15852e8). The original generative pretraining objective does not determine Vej's decision-oriented output interface.

  1. Public pilot. 4,096 training and 256 development decisions; two epochs, 512 updates, selected at step 256.
  2. Expanded training. 32,768 training and 2,048 development decisions; two epochs, selected at step 1,280 with validation NLL 0.636590.
  3. First 100k stage. 100,002 training and 28,572 validation decisions. An unexpected system reboot interrupted training; the saved selected checkpoint was step 1,024 with validation NLL 0.492022.
  4. Recovery stage. Initialized with a fresh optimizer state, seed 43, across one shuffled epoch of the same corpus (6,251 updates). Step 6,144 was selected by lowest pooled validation NLL of 0.458497.
  5. Final evaluation. The selected checkpoint was tested against all 14,286 frozen test decisions on 26 September 2026.

Recovery repeated example exposure rather than performing an exact optimizer resume. These training stages overlap; their counts cannot be summed to claim unique training volume. A larger pool of 530,762 candidate rows was prepared and screened, but not all rows were trained on.

Vej workflows represent authored cases covering customer support, security, billing, and agent traces. Public sources supply natural language inference relations, intent classifications, human preference comparisons, and task dialogue state.

Roles & exposure

A split is a protocol.

A split is a rule about what each slice of the data may be used for, not just three files. Training updates model parameters. Validation repeatedly evaluates checkpoints to select the best step by pooled NLL. The final test was evaluated exactly once after checkpoint selection was finalized, with no temperature scaling, threshold fitting, or checkpoint selection performed on test results. No post-hoc calibration artifact is applied.

These roles represent Vej's protocol derived from source training payloads, not the original publishers' official benchmark splits. The test set consists of 5,000 HelpSteer1 ratings and 9,286 Schema-Guided Dialogue decisions. For SGD, Flights and Hotels families supply test rows, while Events, Services, and Homes supply validation. Because both HelpSteer1 and SGD also supply training examples, this evaluation measures source and domain transfer, not a clean unseen-source benchmark.

Grouping by dialogue and prompt, deterministic selection, length screening, and exact string overlap checks were enforced. These checks preserve specific boundaries; they do not prove conceptual independence.

Known exposure boundaries: twelve inherited WANLI validation decisions had exposure in earlier training lineages. Another historical component retains 19 training decisions without new held-out selections. Base model pretraining overlap is unknown. One test example was inspected during initial comparator setup. The entire test set is now exposed for future model development.

The training corpus contains 68,719 Choice, 13,771 Noul, and 17,512 Score decisions. Validation contains 19,526 Choice, 4,182 Noul, and 4,864 Score decisions. These proportions differ from the final test mixture; consequently, validation accuracy (81.70%) and final test accuracy (76.60%) do not measure matched distributions.

Final test / Qwen 2B alpha2

What was measured.

Exact label accuracy on 14,286 frozen decisions evaluated on 26 September 2026. This test measures categorical decision accuracy. It is not end-to-end tool execution, and it is not Jev's own benchmark.

Score needs more work.

Vej achieves 51.62% exact-level accuracy on Score, compared to 48.44% for a post-hoc test-majority control. That control is calculated from test labels post-hoc and is not a deployable baseline.

Tool readiness is not permission.

In separate development diagnostics, Vej scored 30 of 48 complete tool cases, with nine incorrect calls among 31 ready predictions. Predicting that a tool is ready is an informational output, not authorization to execute.

Calibration and confidence distribution

Across the final test, overall negative log-likelihood is 0.589448, summed Brier score is 0.313870, and descriptive ten-bin expected calibration error (ECE) is 0.053473. No post-hoc calibration was applied. Decisions with maximum probability ≥ 0.9 cover 58.48% of the test set with an error rate of 5.53%. The 0.8 to 0.9 confidence bin shows 59.22% accuracy at 85.16% mean confidence. These descriptive metrics do not establish safe automated execution thresholds.

Hosted comparison / Same test rows

Vej and Jev on the same rows.

Jev is TypeSafe's hosted decision model and API. To evaluate how Vej compares with Jev's decision contract, hosted Jev 1.13 was tested against the exact same 14,286 held-out decisions. Across the full sample, Vej achieved 76.60% exact accuracy (10,943 correct) compared to 71.76% (10,251 correct) for hosted Jev 1.13 using Jev's documented post-launch rounding adjustment. Vej leads overall and on Choice and Score; Jev leads on Noul. The 4.84 percentage point difference reflects this fixed test sample, not a claim of general superiority, broader transfer, or statistical significance.

Strict format validity versus prediction accuracy

Jev returned 14,188 strictly valid responses out of 14,286, representing 99.31% format validity. Strict validity measures adherence to response formatting, not prediction correctness. On those 14,188 strictly valid rows, Jev scored 71.95% accuracy while Vej scored 76.80% on the exact same rows. Counting all 98 non-normalized responses as incorrect yields an overall accuracy of 71.45% for Jev. On strict subsets, Jev scored 83.09% on Choice (6,174 rows), 93.65% on Noul (3,089 rows), and 44.37% on Score (4,925 rows).

The supplemental rounding adjustment

All 98 strict failures were complete response maps whose probabilities summed to 0.99 due to two-decimal output precision. Dividing these maps by their sum restored normalization to 1.0 without changing argmax selections. This adjustment recovered 23 Choice and 75 Score rows, leaving zero unrecoverable responses. No requests were retried and raw records were not overwritten.

What makes this comparison valid and what limits it

The comparison matched dataset hash, row order, question identifiers, target labels, and candidate ordering. Each question was evaluated in a separate hosted request with full state and no local truncation. Noul probability p was represented as [p, 1-p]. Both models resolved argmax ties by candidate order and computed expected scores outside the model using zero-based indices. Jev's two-decimal outputs can create ties and zeros; renormalizing does not restore lost precision. Hosted latency includes network transit, rate limits, and retries, so no serving speed or cost comparison was made.

Development diagnostics

Where tool use falls short.

Separate development suites were used to evaluate multi-step workflow actions and tool call readiness.

  • Across 64 complete workflow actions, Vej completed 60 actions correctly, but made two critical errors where the wrong action was selected.
  • On 48 harder tool diagnostic cases, Vej completed 30 cases, with nine incorrect calls among 31 ready predictions.
  • On 12 original tool cases, Vej completed 4 cases, with three incorrect calls among seven ready predictions.

These diagnostics represent internal development suites, not a clean held-out test. Evaluating intent classification and parameter presence does not measure global tool routing, permission to act, or real-world API success.
